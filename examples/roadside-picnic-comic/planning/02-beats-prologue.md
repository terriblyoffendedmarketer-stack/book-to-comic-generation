# Prologue — Beat Map: Pilman Radio Interview

## Overview
Pure dialogue — a radio interview between a journalist and Dr. Valentine Pilman, Nobel physicist. This is world-building exposition disguised as a conversation. Pilman is dry, evasive, intellectually honest, and slightly amused. The interviewer is eager and keeps trying to steer toward sensationalism. The comedy is in the gap between their agendas.

## Scene 1: Opening — "Not Really a Discovery"
- **Location:** Harmont Radio studio (or could be visualized as split panels — Pilman at home, interviewer in studio)
- **Characters:** Dr. Valentine Pilman, Radio Interviewer
- **What happens:** Interviewer opens by flattering Pilman about the Pilman Radiant. Pilman immediately deflates it — says it wasn't first, wasn't serious, wasn't really his, and wasn't really a discovery. Sets the tone: this man will not play along.
- **Key dialogue to preserve:**
  - Interviewer: "I suppose that your first serious discovery, Dr. Pilman, should be considered what is now called the Pilman Radiant?"
  - Pilman: "I don't think so. The Pilman Radiant wasn't the first, nor was it serious, nor was it really a discovery. And it wasn't completely mine, either."
  - Pilman: "According to some sources, the Pilman Radiant was discovered by a schoolboy."
- **Concepts to explain:** The Pilman Radiant exists. It's connected to the Visitation. Pilman is famous for it.
- **Visual moments:** Pilman's dismissive posture. The interviewer's deflated face.
- **Emotional arc:** Anticipation → deflation. Comedy beat.

## Scene 2: The Pilman Radiant Explained
- **Location:** Same (but visual opportunity for a diagram/illustration panel)
- **Characters:** Pilman, Interviewer
- **What happens:** Pilman explains the Radiant using the spinning globe/bullet metaphor. Six Visitation Zones lie on a curve pointing toward Deneb. This is the first real piece of world-building.
- **Key dialogue to preserve:**
  - Pilman: "Imagine that you spin a huge globe and you start firing bullets into it. The bullet holes would lie on the surface in a smooth curve."
  - Pilman: "All six Visitation Zones are situated on the surface of our planet as though someone had taken six shots at Earth from a pistol located somewhere along the Earth-Deneb line."
  - Interviewer: "My fellow Harmonites! Finally we have heard a clear explanation of the Pilman Radiant!"
- **Concepts to explain:** SIX Zones exist worldwide. They align along a line pointing to the star Deneb. This implies the aliens came from that direction. This is set 30 years after the Visitation.
- **Visual moments:** DIAGRAM PANEL — a spinning globe with six dots connected by a curve, a line extending to a star. This should be a full-width panel with clean infographic style.
- **Emotional arc:** Curiosity → wonder. The reader's first "holy shit" moment about the scale of this world.

## Scene 3: "The Most Important Discovery"
- **Location:** Same
- **Characters:** Pilman, Interviewer
- **What happens:** Interviewer asks about the most important discovery in 30 years of Zone research. Pilman says: the Visitation itself. Not the technology, not the artifacts — just knowing we're not alone. The interviewer is confused and disappointed — he wanted something practical.
- **Key dialogue to preserve:**
  - Interviewer: "What, in your opinion, has been the most important discovery in these thirty years?"
  - Pilman: "The fact of the Visitation itself."
  - Interviewer: "I beg your pardon?"
  - Pilman: "The fact of the Visitation itself is the most important discovery not only of the past thirty years but of the entire history of mankind. It's not so important to know just who these visitors were. It's not important to know where they came from, why they came, why they spent so little time here, or where they disappeared to since. The important thing is that humanity now knows for sure: we are not alone in the universe."
- **Concepts to explain:** The aliens came and LEFT. They didn't stay. Humanity is still trying to understand what happened. The Institute of Extraterrestrial Cultures studies the Zones.
- **Visual moments:** Pilman leaning forward, serious for the first time. Close-up on his face.
- **Emotional arc:** The philosophical weight lands. This is the book's thesis in miniature.

## Scene 4: Stalkers and Leaks
- **Location:** Same
- **Characters:** Pilman, Interviewer
- **What happens:** The conversation shifts to the practical reality — stalkers steal from the Zones, materials leak to "irresponsible persons and organizations." Pilman's role on the UN Commission is about managing this leakage. The interviewer gets excited about stalkers; Pilman is indifferent.
- **Key dialogue to preserve:**
  - Interviewer: "You probably mean stalkers!"
  - Pilman: "I don't know what they are."
  - Interviewer: "That's what we in Harmont call the thieves who risk their lives in the Zone to grab everything they can lay their hands on. It's become a whole new profession."
  - Pilman: "I understand. No, that's not within our competence."
  - Pilman: "There is a steady leak of materials from the Visitation Zones into the hands of irresponsible persons and organizations."
- **Concepts to explain:** STALKERS — people who illegally enter Zones to steal alien artifacts. It's a profession in Harmont. The government/UN tries to control Zone access but can't fully.
- **Visual moments:** Could cut away from the interview to show a shadowy stalker figure at the Zone perimeter — first visual hint of the world we're about to enter.
- **Emotional arc:** The word "stalker" lands. The reader now knows the protagonist's profession before meeting him.

## Scene 5: Closing — The Deflection
- **Location:** Same
- **Characters:** Pilman, Interviewer
- **What happens:** Pilman tries to change the subject to the arts ("Can't we talk about the arts instead?"). The interviewer pushes back. Pilman concedes he might visit Harmont one day. The interview ends with gentle comedy.
- **Key dialogue to preserve:**
  - Pilman: "Can't we talk about the arts instead? Wouldn't the listeners care to know my opinion of the incomparable Gôdi Müller?"
  - Interviewer: "Then, we can hope that one fine day Harmonites will see their famous fellow citizen on the streets of his home town?"
  - Pilman: "It's not impossible."
- **Concepts to explain:** None new. Pilman is from Harmont — he's a local who left.
- **Visual moments:** Pilman's wry smile. Maybe a pull-back shot showing the radio studio, microphone between them.
- **Emotional arc:** Light, warm ending. Contrast with the darkness about to come in Chapter 1.

## Estimated Comic Pages: 3 pages
- Page 1: Scenes 1-2 (opening + Radiant explanation with diagram)
- Page 2: Scene 3 (the "most important discovery" — give it room to breathe)
- Page 3: Scenes 4-5 (stalkers + closing)

## Design Notes
- This is ALL dialogue — no action, no narration. Use the interview format visually:
  - Two-shot panels (both characters)
  - Alternating close-ups (talking heads)
  - One diagram/infographic panel for the Pilman Radiant
  - Maybe one "cutaway" panel showing a Zone silhouette when stalkers are mentioned
- Tone: warm, intellectual, slightly comic. Contrast with Ch 1's grit.
- The Radiant diagram is the visual centerpiece — make it a full-width panel.
