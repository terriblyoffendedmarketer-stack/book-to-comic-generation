# Roadside Picnic - Chapter 2 Beat Map
## "Redrick Schuhart, Age 28, Married, No Permanent Occupation"

---

## Chapter Overview

Five years after Chapter 1. Red Schuhart is now 28, married to Guta, with a daughter nicknamed Monkey -- a child mutated by the Zone, covered in golden fur. He has left the institute and become a full-time criminal stalker. This chapter is a single brutal night-to-morning-to-afternoon cycle: Red returns from a Zone run with the injured Burbridge, dodges patrols, sells his haul to foreign buyers, gets ambushed by the police, escapes, and surrenders himself -- all while the world presses in on his family. The chapter has no Zone scenes; instead, the Zone's presence is felt through its consequences: Burbridge's dissolved legs, Monkey's mutation, the dead walking home, and the porcelain container of "invincible and inevitable death."

### Narrative Arc

1. **Cemetery escape** -- Red waits out a patrol car while hiding with the injured Burbridge among gravestones; a zombie wanders through and scares the patrol away
2. **The drive home** -- Loading Burbridge, avoiding roadblocks, passing the zombie on the road, navigating mud and suburbs
3. **Home and family** -- Guta's embrace, Monkey sleeping, domestic warmth as counterweight to darkness
4. **Sorting the swag** -- Red catalogs his haul, discovers the mysterious porcelain container, hesitates
5. **The Metropole deal** -- Selling to Throaty and Bones in a luxury hotel; the spinning hoop (perpetual motion); the camera assignment
6. **Dina Burbridge** -- Delivering money and news to Buzzard's daughter; her savage reaction
7. **The Borscht trap** -- Ernest has betrayed him; Captain Quarterblad's ambush; Red's daring trapdoor escape
8. **Surrender** -- Hiding the porcelain, calling Guta, calling Throaty, walking to turn himself in

### Main Themes

- **The cost of stalking on family.** Every scene with Guta and Monkey is shot through with tenderness and guilt. The neighbors shun them. Monkey is losing her humanity.
- **Moral ambiguity.** Red almost kills Burbridge with brass knuckles, then carries him to safety. He despises the buyers but sells to them. He hides the deadly porcelain instead of destroying it.
- **The dead returning.** The zombie in the cemetery and the cab driver's story about a dead man coming home establish that the Zone is leaking into the world.
- **Sensory invasion.** Red's hallucinatory episode outside the Metropole -- smelling a million odors, feeling space become solid -- shows the Zone has permanently changed him.
- **Entrapment.** Red is trapped by the Zone's economy: he has no legitimate occupation, no way to emigrate, and the law is closing in.

### Page Estimate

At 5 panels per page, max 6: **22-28 comic pages**

This is a long, dense chapter with 13 distinct scenes. The cemetery opening, the home scenes, the hotel deal, and the Borscht escape are the set pieces that need the most room. Several transitional scenes (driving, cab ride, garage work) can be compressed.

## Scene 1: The Cemetery — Waiting

**Location:** Cemetery on the edge of the Zone. Night, post-rain. Gravestones, lopsided crosses, ash trees, a broken ten-foot wall. Searchlights sweeping from a patrol car parked on the highway.

**Characters:** Red Schuhart (28, in waterproof jumpsuit, lying behind a gravestone), Burbridge ("Buzzard," elderly stalker, lying injured in a gap in the wall)

**What happens:** Red lies behind a gravestone, watching a patrol car's searchlights comb the cemetery. Two hours of waiting. He spots -- or imagines -- a dark figure sitting motionless on a grave, white face with black sunken eye-holes. This is a zombie, one of the dead returned from the Zone. Red crawls to Burbridge, who is lying in a break in the wall with his legs destroyed by "witches' jelly." He gives the old man whiskey.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "You came back ... Good fellow ... Red. You won't leave an old man to die."
- "You were always a rat. A son of a bitch." / "A rat. That's true. You can't get by without being one."
- "Bob the Gorilla blew it. Pharaoh the Banker kicked the bucket. And what a stalker he was! And he was killed. Slimy, too. And Norman Four-Eyes. Culligan. Pete the Scab. All of them. I'm the only one who's survived."
- "Have you heard about the Golden Ball?" / "Yes." / "You think it's a fairy tale?"
- "I'll give you everything. The Golden Ball. I'll draw you a map. Show you all the traps. I'll tell you everything."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **Witches' jelly (colloidal gas):** A Zone substance that dissolves living tissue. Burbridge stepped in it -- his legs below the knee are now boneless, "like a rubber stick. You could tie knots in it."
- **The Golden Ball:** A legendary Zone artifact said to grant wishes. First major mention. Burbridge claims to have found it and says it gave him survival, health, and "good children."
- **Zombies / moulages:** The dead are returning from the cemetery near the Zone. The figure Red sees is one of these -- walking blind, arms outstretched, stumbling into crosses. They walk toward town.
- **Lead-lined raincoat:** Stalkers wear protective gear. The Zone requires specialized equipment.
- **Stalker mortality:** Burbridge lists dead stalkers by nickname -- this is a profession with an enormous death toll.

**Strong visual moments:**
- FULL-WIDTH OPENER: Red lying prone behind a gravestone, searchlight beam sweeping inches above him, rain-wet grass gleaming. Establish noir atmosphere.
- The zombie: a bent black figure sitting against a marble obelisk, white face with black holes for eyes, seen in a split-second flash of searchlight.
- Burbridge's face in flickering blue light -- wide-open glassy eyes, dark stubble. "His upturned face looked dead."
- Red feeling Burbridge's leg below the knee: "like a rubber stick."

**Emotional arc:** Tension (patrol), dread (zombie), grim pragmatism (assessing Burbridge). Red is calm, cold, and in control despite everything.

**Estimated panels:** 12-15 (3 pages)

## Scene 2: The Zombie Walks

**Location:** Cemetery, still night. The patrol car's searchlights converge on the marble obelisk.

**Characters:** Red, Burbridge (background), the zombie

**What happens:** The searchlights freeze and converge on the obelisk. Red sees the zombie clearly now -- a bent black figure wandering among crosses, moving blindly with arms outstretched, fingers spread wide. It bumps into a cross, stumbles, walks around it, continues with "bizarre, inhuman stubbornness, like a wind-up toy." It disappears underground, surfaces elsewhere. The patrol panics -- lights go out, engine roars, the car tears away at speed. The way is clear.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "They've gone away. Red, let's go. Hurry!" / "Let's go, what are you waiting for?"
- Red, still staring at the dark where the zombie walked: "All right. Let's go."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- The border patrol guards are terrified of the Zone and its effects. Even armed soldiers flee from the walking dead.
- The zombie moves mechanically, not aggressively -- but its wrongness is what terrifies.

**Strong visual moments:**
- FULL-WIDTH PANEL: The zombie silhouetted in converging searchlight beams, arms outstretched, fingers spread, surrounded by crosses and gravestones. The defining horror image of this chapter.
- The zombie falling underground and resurfacing elsewhere -- spatial wrongness.
- Patrol car's red and blue lights racing away through shrubs -- sudden evacuation of light, plunging everything into darkness.

**Emotional arc:** Horror building to a peak, then sudden release. The zombie clears their path. The terrible becomes useful.

**Estimated panels:** 8-10 (2 pages)

## Scene 3: The Escape — Through the Cemetery and to the Jeep

**Location:** Cemetery wall, highway, roadside. Pre-dawn.

**Characters:** Red, Burbridge

**What happens:** Red lifts Burbridge and crawls through the hole in the wall. The old man is "unbearably heavy, like a corpse." Red is terrified of bumping into the zombie in the dark. They reach the highway at dawn. Red finds the camouflaged jeep, sweeps branches off, loads Burbridge. He checks for tracks and smokes his first cigarette. Burbridge screams about his knees. Red decides to take the southern suburbs route to avoid roadblocks.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Let's go, let's go. Don't worry, I've got the swag, I won't let go. Come on!"
- "Hurry up! Hurry. My knees, I still have my knees. If only we could save my knees!"
- "Don't be a jerk, Buzzard. We can't go straight through town. There are three roadblocks. We'll get stopped once for sure."
- "They'll take one look at your feet and it's curtains." / "And what if they feel your legs?" / "Feel them. I'll yell so loud that they'll never try feeling a leg again."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- Red is methodical even under extreme pressure -- checking for tracks, picking up cigarette butts, thinking about evidence.
- The hidden compartment in the jeep (false gas tank) for smuggling swag.
- The cover story: two fishermen returning from a not-very-successful trip. Burbridge even bought fish beforehand.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Red crawling on all fours through the wall gap with Burbridge clinging to his neck -- primal, bestial struggle.
- Pre-dawn landscape: birds making uncertain noises, night gloom turning blue over black houses. Chilly, damp, transitional light.
- Red checking the road shoulder with a flashlight, methodical as a crime scene technician.

**Emotional arc:** Desperate physical exertion, then cold professionalism. Red transforms from animal survivor back into careful operative.

**Estimated panels:** 8-10 (2 pages)

## Scene 4: The Drive — Zombie on the Road

**Location:** Highway along the Zone wall, then mud roads through abandoned suburbs. Pre-dawn to dawn.

**Characters:** Red (driving), Burbridge (back seat, moaning)

**What happens:** Red drives without headlights along the Zone wall. A bent figure appears in the middle of the road -- the zombie, headed for town "like all of them." Red hunches over the wheel and passes it without slowing down. Burbridge prays in terror. Red turns onto a rutted back road. Burbridge wails about his legs. Red slaps him across the face to shut him up. They bounce through abandoned summer-house villages -- once-beautiful places now desolate. One inhabited house remains, a furious dog chasing them.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Mother of God! Red, did you see that?" / "Yes."
- "Where are you going? You'll ruin my legs, you bastard!" (Red slaps him.)
- Burbridge "crying, sobbing, and snuffling ... complaining and threatening, but in a very quiet and indistinct voice ... Something about legs, knees, and his darling Archie. Then he shut up."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- Zombies walk toward town -- they return to where they lived. This is a pattern, not a one-off event.
- The Zone has an expanding dead zone around it -- suburban villages abandoned, only one inhabited house remaining.

**Strong visual moments:**
- The zombie in the headlights: a bent figure striding rhythmically in the middle of the road, arms outstretched. Red hunched over the wheel, passing it on the left. This should be a wide panel, headlights casting the figure in stark relief.
- Red's hand connecting with Burbridge's stubbled cheek -- a quick brutal act of necessary silencing.
- The one inhabited house: yellow window through drawn blinds, wet laundry on the line, a huge dog chasing them through mud spray. A flicker of normalcy in the wasteland.

**Emotional arc:** Sustained tension, punctuated by Red's violence toward Burbridge. The landscape mirrors their deterioration.

**Estimated panels:** 8-10 (2 pages)

## Scene 5: The Checkpoint and the Brass Knuckles

**Location:** Overlooking Western Highway checkpoint. Dawn. A rickety bridge, bushes, a little trailer house with lighted windows and a patrol car.

**Characters:** Red, Burbridge

**What happens:** Red stops the car and walks to scout the checkpoint. He sees the patrol guards are warming up inside their trailer. He finds brass knuckles in his pocket, slips his fingers into them. He walks back to the car. Burbridge sits up and they stare at each other. Red considers killing him -- then lets the brass knuckles drop back into his pocket. Instead, he tells Burbridge to play unconscious.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "The toads." (Red, watching the checkpoint.)
- Burbridge, staring at Red, says "distinctly": "The map ... all the traps, everything ... You'll find it and you won't be sorry."
- "All right. All you have to do is lie there in a faint. Understand? Moan and don't let anyone touch you."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- Red genuinely considered killing Burbridge. The brass knuckles were his weapon. This is the moral nadir of the opening sequence -- he chose mercy, but barely.
- The checkpoint is routine Zone-perimeter security. They pass by driving slowly and making all the correct signals.

**Strong visual moments:**
- CLOSE-UP: Red's white knuckles wrapped around brass knuckles in his pocket. This is the chapter's first moral crisis -- make the reader see the decision.
- The two men staring at each other silently in growing dawn light. Burbridge's face: "even older than usual, wrinkled, bald, unshaven, and with rotten teeth." He knows.
- Red loosening his fingers, the brass knuckles dropping into his pocket. The moment of mercy.

**Emotional arc:** This is the chapter's first emotional peak. Red's capacity for violence is real and present. His mercy is not sentimental -- it's pragmatic and reluctant. The tension between these defines his character.

**Estimated panels:** 6-8 (1-2 pages)

## Scene 6: Home — Guta, Monkey, and Domestic Life

**Location:** Red and Guta's apartment. Morning. Entry hall, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom.

**Characters:** Red, Guta (his wife, strong and beautiful), Monkey (their daughter, ~3-4 years old, covered in golden fur, huge dark eyes with no whites)

**What happens:** Red arrives home. Guta throws her arms around him, her heart pounding. He gives her the fish (cover story). He peeks into the bedroom where Monkey sleeps -- strokes her back, amazed by the golden fur's silkiness. He showers, scrubbing himself red. He dresses. Monkey wakes and ambushes him -- grabbing his leg, babbling nonstop about the neighbor's boy, a kitten, porridge, Uncle Gutalin being drunk, why fish don't drown. A mysterious phone call: silence, then a click. Red dresses in his suit and leaves, kissing Guta and Monkey.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "All right. I'll have the coffee ready in a minute." / "I've brought some fish. Fry it up, won't you, I'm starved."
- "How's Monkey?" / "She was babbling all evening. She keeps asking, where's daddy, where's daddy?"
- Guta, about the neighbors: "The woman from below knocked at our door last night. Her eyes were bulging and she was practically spitting with anger. Why are we sawing in the bathroom in the middle of the night?"
- "Listen, maybe we should move? Buy a house somewhere out in the country, where there's no one else?" / "What about Monkey?" / "God, don't you think the two of us could make her life good?" / Guta shakes her head. "She loves children. And they love her. It's not their fault that..."
- "Some husband you are now. You're just an empty bag, not a husband. You have to be stuffed first." / "What if I could? Miracles do happen, you know." / "I haven't seen miracles like that from you before."
- Monkey: "Ha!" (grabbing his leg) -- then nonstop babble including "Gutalin's smashed again" and "Why don't fish drown if they live in water?" and "Why are there five fingers, and only two hands, and only one nose?"
- "Monkey. My little Monkey. You sweet little Monkey, you."
- Guta in her favorite blue dress: "You'd better go." / "What about me? Kiss me?" (Monkey, pushing between them)
- "Nonsense. Don't worry. I'll call."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **Monkey's mutation:** She has golden fur covering her body, huge dark eyes with no whites. She's the result of Zone mutation passed to stalkers' children. The neighbors shun the family because of her.
- **The neighbor hostility:** The building is emptying out because of Monkey. People are scared.
- **Kirill's posthumous article:** Red reads the Reports of the IIEC and sees Kirill Panov's name framed in black -- his friend from Chapter 1, killed during an experiment. He tosses the journal away.
- **Gutalin:** Red's friend, mentioned -- a drunk, but loved by Monkey.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Guta throwing her arms around Red in the doorway, face buried in his chest. He stands patient and exhausted, still in his jumpsuit. Warmth against grime.
- Red stroking Monkey's furry back as she sleeps. CLOSE-UP on his hand against golden fur. "Amazed for the thousandth time by the fur's silkiness and length."
- Monkey pouncing on Red, climbing all over him. Her face: huge dark eyes with no whites, plump cheeks covered in golden fleece. She is simultaneously adorable and alien.
- Guta in the blue dress, low-cut, makeup done, hair done. Standing in the hallway seeing him off. The normalcy of it.
- Red on the landing, confronting the fat neighbor in striped pajamas. Polite menace.

**Emotional arc:** This is the warm heart of the chapter. Tenderness, domesticity, love -- but everything underscored by wrongness. Monkey's mutation. The hostile neighbors. The emptying building. Kirill's death notice. Red is trying to hold together a life that the Zone is slowly destroying.

**Estimated panels:** 12-15 (3 pages)

## Scene 7: Sorting the Swag

**Location:** Red's storeroom. Morning.

**Characters:** Red (alone)

**What happens:** Red puts on rubber apron and gloves and catalogs his haul on the storeroom table: empties, pins, batteries, bracelets, a mysterious hoop, black sprays, sponges, itchers, carbonated clay. He tests the pins in the dark -- twelve out of seventy-three "speak" (emit colored light pulses). He calculates prices. Then he confronts the porcelain container -- the most dangerous object. He stares at it, smokes a whole cigarette without touching it, and then decisively stuffs it back into the bag. "What the hell! That's it. Enough."

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "What the hell!" (stuffing the container back) "That's it. Enough."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **The swag taxonomy:** Empties (batteries that multiply), pins (emit light signals that "mean something, maybe something very important"), black sprays, bracelets, sponges, itchers, carbonated clay -- these are all Zone artifacts with varying known/unknown uses.
- **The porcelain container:** Contains "invincible and inevitable death." This is the witches' jelly or something equally catastrophic. It's the most dangerous thing Red has ever handled, and he's terrified of it.
- **The hoop:** A white metal ring, slightly larger than a bracelet. Its significance will become clear later -- it's a perpetual motion device.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Red in rubber apron and gloves under harsh electric light, Zone artifacts spread on a table. He looks like a chemist in a clandestine lab.
- Testing pins in the dark: "weak red flashes ran along the pin and were suddenly replaced by slower green pulses." Beautiful sci-fi imagery -- tiny alien lights in darkness.
- Red sitting motionless, staring at the porcelain container, cigarette burning down. The moment of temptation and retreat.

**Emotional arc:** Professional calm dissolving into dread. The porcelain container is a moral test -- Red knows what it could do. He doesn't open it, but he doesn't destroy it either.

**Estimated panels:** 6-8 (1-2 pages)

## Scene 8: The Cafe and Noonan

**Location:** Small cafe across from the Metropole hotel. Morning, approaching 9 AM. Awning tables, undercover police eating hot dogs, a sergeant eating French fries.

**Characters:** Red, Richard Noonan (supply representative, short, plump, pink, cheerful)

**What happens:** Red sits under an awning, watching the Metropole, drinking coffee. He spots Noonan emerging from the hotel and tries to hide, but Noonan sees him and comes over, irrepressibly friendly. They exchange pleasantries. Noonan offers Red his old job back at the institute. Red refuses -- "a second Kirill hasn't been born" and "the lab assistants are paid peanuts." Noonan hints at a job offer. Red is evasive, checking his watch. Noonan leaves.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Hey! Schuhart! Red!" (Noonan's hearty call from his Peugeot)
- "What are you doing here at the crack of dawn? I haven't seen you in a hundred years."
- "Nothing special. Just unimportant things."
- "Listen, Red, why don't you drop your unimportant things and come back to the institute." / "Nope, a second Kirill hasn't been born. Anyway, there's nothing for me to do in your institute. It's all automated now, you have robots going into the Zone and that means that the robots get all the bonuses. The lab assistants are paid peanuts. It wouldn't even keep me in cigarettes."
- "I don't like having things arranged for me. I've taken care of myself all my life, and I intend to keep on doing it."
- "A man needs money so that he doesn't have to always be counting it."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **Noonan's role:** Appears to be an equipment supplier for the institute. Actually (as revealed in Chapter 3) an intelligence agent. The reader doesn't know this yet -- he seems like a friendly businessman.
- **Robot stalkers:** The institute now sends machines into the Zone instead of people. Legitimate work in the Zone has been automated.
- **Kirill reference:** Red's reason for leaving the institute -- his friend and mentor Kirill is dead.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Noonan's Peugeot: "also plump, short, freshly washed, and seemingly confident that no unpleasantness threatened it." Car as mirror of owner -- a visual joke.
- The tableau of undercover cops and uniformed soldiers eating fast food at tiny cafe tables, Red among them trying to be invisible. Everyone is watching everyone.
- Red covering his face, hunching over his cup -- the criminal trying not to be seen by the straight world.

**Emotional arc:** Comic relief. Noonan is warmth and normalcy, everything Red isn't. But Red is too deep in his criminal life to come back. The offer of legitimate work is a door he can no longer walk through.

**Estimated panels:** 6-8 (1-2 pages)

## Scene 9: The Sensory Attack and the Metropole Deal

**Location:** Street outside the Metropole, then elevator, then Room 874. Mid-morning.

**Characters:** Red, Throaty (Hugh -- foreign buyer, puffy face, blue angelic eyes, never blinks), Bones (Phil -- Throaty's nervous partner, huge pigeon-toed feet)

**What happens:** Walking to the hotel, Red is hit by an overwhelming sensory episode: "a million odors cascaded in on him at once," space becomes solid with edges and corners. It lasts a second. He staggers, a cop offers help. He enters the Metropole -- luxury, wealth, people wearing Zone artifacts as jewelry. In Room 874, Throaty is clipping a cigar. Red reports Burbridge is in the hospital. Bones is angry about Red talking to Noonan outside. They do business: Red presents his list, demands payment first, gets two packs of cash. During inspection, Bones discovers the hoop spins perpetually on his finger -- a perpetual motion device. Throaty pays a bonus and demands more. Then Throaty lays out the real mission: they need photos from deep in the Zone and the porcelain container filled. "And you'll never have to go into the Zone again."

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Punctuality is the politeness of kings. Good day, young man!"
- "Burbridge isn't coming." / "Good old Burbridge. Old Burbridge's nerves are acting up."
- "We've told you a hundred times. No contacts before a meeting. And what do you do?" / "I say hello. And you?"
- "And why do I love you so much, my son? And they say love at first sight doesn't exist!" (Throaty)
- "Phil, buddy, how do they say it here? Dole out the cabbage, lay some greenbacks on him ... and give me a match."
- "What about the most important thing?" / "No way." / "How about you, Phil?" / "You're throwing dust in our eyes, Schuhart. Why the mystery, I ask you?" / "That comes with the territory: shady dealings. We're in a demanding profession."
- Bones: "Hey, Hugh, look at this! It's not stopping!" (the spinning hoop)
- "Do you know what a perpetuum mobile is?" / "Nope, we never studied that."
- Throaty, final: "All this is very nice, charming, and so on, and the hoop is simply marvelous, but above all we need two things: the photos and the container filled up. Return our camera to us, but with exposed film, and our porcelain container, but not empty. Filled. And you'll never have to go into the Zone again."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **The sensory episode:** Red experiences something from the Zone outside the Zone. Space becomes tangible, smells multiply. The Zone has changed him permanently. "It wasn't a different world -- it was this world turning a new, unknown side to him."
- **Throaty and Bones (Hugh and Phil):** Foreign black-market buyers of Zone artifacts. Probably intelligence operatives or arms dealers. They want military applications -- the porcelain container (a weapon) and reconnaissance photos.
- **The perpetual motion hoop:** A Zone artifact that violates thermodynamics. Once spinning, it doesn't stop. Worth a fortune.
- **The porcelain container:** The buyers want it filled with witches' jelly -- a substance that dissolves everything it touches. This is weapons procurement.
- **The elevator scene:** A necklace made of black sprays set in silver -- Zone artifacts have become luxury fashion accessories. The Zone is being commodified.

**Strong visual moments:**
- THE SENSORY ATTACK: Full-width panel. Red staggering on the sidewalk, the world fracturing around him into geometric shapes -- "huge stiff balloons, slippery pyramids, gigantic prickly crystals." This is the chapter's most psychedelic moment. Render as distorted, kaleidoscopic reality.
- Throaty's blue angelic eyes that never blink. Extreme close-up.
- The hoop spinning on Bones's finger like a propeller, not stopping. Bones staring at it pop-eyed.
- Red counting money, chin resting on his briefcase, while Throaty watches with a frozen smile. Two predators sizing each other up.
- The woman in the elevator wearing a necklace of black sprays set in silver -- Zone artifacts as jewelry.

**Emotional arc:** The sensory attack is terrifying and revelatory. The hotel deal is a chess match -- Red is sharp and transactional but also exhausted. The final demand (fill the porcelain) raises the stakes enormously. Red is being asked to procure a weapon of mass destruction.

**Estimated panels:** 14-18 (3-4 pages)

## Scene 10: Dina Burbridge

**Location:** Buzzard Burbridge's cottage -- two stories, glassed-in veranda, garden, gazebo under apple trees. A red mat on the lawn.

**Characters:** Red, Dina Burbridge (Buzzard's daughter, stunning, in a tiny bathing suit), Hamster (Dixon, an old crippled servant, gnarled and trembling)

**What happens:** Red arrives at Burbridge's house and is met by Hamster, a Zone-crippled servant who can barely walk. Dina calls from the garden. She's lounging on a red mat, drinking, nearly naked. Red tells her both her father's legs will be amputated. Her smile freezes. He throws a pack of money at her feet. Then she erupts -- not in grief for her father, but in fury that Red dragged Burbridge out instead of leaving him to die. She calls Red a fool, says Burbridge has them all "wrapped around his finger," and mocks the promise of the Golden Ball. Red slaps her. She sinks down, still calling him a fool.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Hi, Red! Where's the old man? Don't tell me he's messed up again?"
- "Burbridge is in the hospital. They're going to amputate his legs." / "Both legs?" / "Both. Maybe below the knees, maybe above."
- "Too bad. And that means you ..."
- "You want?" (Dina, offering the glass -- then: "She waited just long enough for him to get the double entendre and then handed him the glass.")
- Hamster bringing vodka: Red says, "Thanks, Dixon. That's just what I need right now. As usual, you're right on top of things."
- "And so you dragged him out. You carried him, you jerk, through the whole Zone, you redheaded cretin, you dragged that bastard on your backbone, you ass. You blew an opportunity like that."
- "He's got all of you idiots wrapped around his finger. He'll walk all over your bones. Just wait and see, he'll walk on your thick skulls on crutches."
- "I'll bet he promised you the Golden Ball, right? The map, the traps, right? Jerk! I can see by your dumb face that he did! Just wait, he'll give you a map. Lord have mercy on the soul of the redheaded fool Redrick Schuhart."
- After Red slaps her: "You fool ... Red. To blow an opportunity like that."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **Dina's character:** Burbridge "wished himself up some marvelous children" -- she's impossibly beautiful, the product of the Golden Ball's wish. But she's also savage, mercenary, and sees her father with total clarity.
- **Hamster (Dixon):** A Zone-damaged man, nearly immobile, who serves the Burbridge household. His devotion to hospitality -- dragging a glass of vodka across the lawn, sweating and trembling -- is both heartbreaking and darkly comic.
- **The Golden Ball as currency of manipulation:** Dina knows exactly how Burbridge uses the Golden Ball promise to control stalkers. This confirms Red's suspicion and foreshadows Chapter 4.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Dina on the red mat: "all silk and satin, firm and full, flawless ... a hundred-twenty pounds of sugar-candy flesh, and emerald eyes that had an inner glow." Red standing over her in a wrinkled suit with a briefcase. The contrast is obscene.
- Money scattered in a rainbow on the red mat at her naked legs.
- Hamster struggling across the lawn with a glass of vodka, sweat pouring, eyes bulging. "He extended the glass in despair and sort of mooed and howled."
- Dina standing, fists jammed into hips, blocking out the world with her body, screaming at Red.
- Red slapping her. Hard. She sinks to the grass.

**Emotional arc:** Dark comedy turning savage. Dina's reaction is the chapter's most shocking character reveal -- she wanted her father dead. Her fury at Red's mercy is a funhouse mirror of the Burbridge family's monstrousness. Red's slap is not heroic; it's the act of an exhausted man who doesn't want to hear the truth.

**Estimated panels:** 10-12 (2-3 pages)

## Scene 11: The Borscht — Betrayal and Escape

**Location:** The Borscht bar (same as Chapter 1, but now under Ernest's management). Front room, then back room with a trapdoor.

**Characters:** Red, Ernest (bartender, trembling and guilty), Captain Quarterblad (military police, yellow and angry), UN trooper, three undercover men posing as customers, two porters (plainclothes muscle)

**What happens:** Red enters the Borscht half-asleep. Ernest is at the bar, visibly nervous -- his hand trembles pouring beer. "Did you bring it?" The trap closes: the three "customers" block the door, a huge porter blocks the back. Red is forced behind the bar into the back room where Captain Quarterblad waits. They frisk him, find the brass knuckles. Quarterblad opens the briefcase and finds the remaining swag (Red already sold the best stuff). Quarterblad demands Red empty his pockets. Red throws the money down, cursing. Then, while picking up scattered bills on his hands and knees, he spots the brass ring of a trapdoor. He grabs it, pulls, and dives headfirst into the wine cellar. He somersaults, sprints through narrow passages between wine cases, knocking them over behind him, bursts through a door into Ernest's garage, tears through garbage to a hole in the wall, crawls out, and runs through courtyards, tunnels, and fences. He escapes into a parade of two hundred disheveled men from some brotherhood, loses himself in the crowd, and disappears before Quarterblad can cordon the area.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- Ernest, cramped voice: "Did you bring it?" / "Bring what?"
- "Let's go talk." (Ernest, sealing the trap)
- "You bloodsucker." (Red to Ernest, realizing the betrayal)
- Quarterblad: "Anything in your pockets? Empty them."
- "Snakes. Skunks." / "Lousy toads!" / "There you go. I hope you choke on it!"
- Quarterblad: "Very interesting. Now pick it up."
- "The hell I will. Your slaves will pick it up. You can pick it up yourself, for all I care."
- "Pick up the money, stalker." (Quarterblad, leaning his fist on the table)
- "Don't snort at me! You'll lose your snot." (to the gloating trooper)

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **Ernest's betrayal:** Ernest is the bar owner from Chapter 1. He has turned informant -- either pressured or paid by the authorities.
- **Captain Quarterblad:** Military police who have been hunting Red. This is his jurisdiction.
- **The escape route:** Red knows this neighborhood from childhood. The trapdoor leads to a wine cellar, which connects to the garage, which has a pre-prepared hole in the wall. He memorized escape routes years ago. This is how stalkers survive.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Ernest's trembling hand pouring beer, the bottle hitting the mug's edge. His lowered eyelids, twisted mouth. The face of a man who has betrayed someone.
- The trap tightening: men at the door, a huge porter with a mop, Quarterblad behind the curtain. Red realizing he's surrounded.
- FULL-WIDTH PANEL: Red diving headfirst into the open trapdoor, briefcase gone, money scattering in the air. Captain Quarterblad's hand reaching. This is the action set piece of the chapter.

**Emotional arc:** Betrayal hitting like a gut punch, then pure adrenaline. The escape sequence is the chapter's action peak -- physical, desperate, exhilarating. Red's knowledge of the neighborhood saves him. This is stalker skill applied to urban survival.

## Scene 12: The Garage and Monkey — Watching Through the Crack

**Location:** Red's garage (approached from the back), then the phone booth at the abandoned end of Miner Street.

**Characters:** Red (alone; Monkey visible through the crack)

**What happens:** Red waits near his garage while workmen load a van. He enters through a hidden passage, retrieves the paper roll from the swag bag, puts it inside his shirt, grabs an old leather jacket and greasy cap. Before leaving, he looks through a crack in the door and sees Monkey playing with balloons near the swings he built. Three old women watch with pursed lips. But the kids play with her "as though she were just like them." He tears himself away and crawls through the hole.

At the phone booth at the end of Miner Street, he checks his emergency stash: gun, bullets, fake documents, bracelets. Then he takes out the porcelain container and holds it, feeling the nervous tic in his cheek.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Schuhart. What are you doing, you snake? You scum, they can kill us all with this thing."
- "Bastards. You got in my way. I would have thrown it back into the Zone, the bitch, and it would have been all over."
- "All right. Every man for himself, only God takes care of everybody. I've had it."
- "That's it. It's settled."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **The porcelain container's significance:** Red is hiding a weapon of mass destruction under a phone booth at the edge of an abandoned neighborhood. He wanted to throw it back into the Zone but was delayed by the workmen. Now he keeps it as leverage.
- **Red's emergency preparations:** He has pre-positioned escape supplies throughout the city -- gun, fake documents, cash. This is a professional criminal's infrastructure.
- **The swings and the bribery:** Red built playground equipment to buy acceptance for Monkey from the neighborhood children. It works for the kids; the old women still disapprove.

**Strong visual moments:**
- Red peering through the crack in the garage door: a bright sliver of light showing Monkey running with balloons near the swings. Three old women with pursed lips on a bench. This is the chapter's most poignant image -- the father who can't go to his daughter.
- Red kneeling by the phone booth, holding the porcelain container in one hand. His cheek twitching. The abandoned street stretching behind him -- cracked cement, boarded windows, tumbleweed.
- The phone booth tilting as Red pushes it aside to hide the container in the pit beneath.

**Emotional arc:** Aching tenderness (watching Monkey) collapsing into cold calculation (hiding the weapon). Red is making his final arrangements. He knows he's going to prison.

## Scene 13: The Phone Calls — Surrender

**Location:** Phone booth at the end of Miner Street. Hot afternoon. Cracked cement, boarded-up houses, tumbleweed.

**Characters:** Red (in the phone booth), Guta (voice only), Throaty (voice only)

**What happens:** Red makes two phone calls. First to Guta: he tells her he's been caught again, six to eight months, with visiting rights. "The house is clean. Don't worry." He hangs up abruptly, stands with eyes shut and teeth clenched. Then he calls Throaty: tells him where the porcelain container is hidden, demands Throaty take care of Guta financially, warns him never to double-cross him. Throaty offers a lawyer; Red refuses -- "every last cent goes to my wife." Then Red walks up Miner Street between the empty, boarded-up houses, hands in his pockets, to turn himself in.

**Key dialogue to preserve:**
- "Guta. Please, don't worry. They caught me again." ... "It's a minor offense, six to eight months, with visiting rights. We'll manage."
- "Will there be a search?" / "Let them. The house is clean."
- "Don't worry, keep your tail up -- you know, bright-eyed and bushy-tailed. You married a stalker, so don't complain. See you tomorrow. And remember, I didn't call. I kiss your little nose."
- "It's Schuhart. Listen carefully and don't interrupt." / "Schuhart? What Schuhart?" / "Don't interrupt, I said!"
- "They caught me, I ran, and I'm going to turn myself in now. I'm going to get two and a half or three years. My wife will be penniless. You take care of her. So that she needs nothing, understand?"
- "Not far from the place where we first met, there's a phone booth. It's the only one, you won't mistake it. The porcelain is under it."
- "If I come back and find out you double-crossed me ... I don't suggest that you do."
- Throaty: "Maybe you want a lawyer?" / "No. Every last cent goes to my wife. My regards."

**Concepts needing explanation:**
- **Red's calculation:** By turning himself in voluntarily, he gets a lighter sentence (6-8 months instead of 2-3 years for fleeing arrest). But he's using the porcelain container as leverage to ensure Guta is supported while he's in prison.
- **The deal with Throaty:** Red trades the location of a weapon of mass destruction for his wife's financial security. This is the chapter's final moral compromise -- protecting his family by enabling something terrible.

**Strong visual moments:**
- FINAL PAGE SEQUENCE: Red in the phone booth, lit harshly, eyes shut, teeth clenched, after hanging up on Guta. The silence after the call. This should breathe -- give it a full panel.
- The second call: Red's face hard, commanding. "Listen carefully and don't interrupt."
- CLOSING IMAGE (full-width): Red walking away up Miner Street, alone, hands in pockets, between boarded-up houses. Small figure, long road, desolate landscape. He is walking toward prison. The chapter ends here.

**Emotional arc:** Devastating quiet after all the chapter's violence and chaos. Red is finally still. His love for Guta is in every word of the first call -- the forced cheerfulness, the "I kiss your little nose." The call to Throaty is pure business. Then he walks, alone, into consequence. This is the chapter's emotional peak.

**Estimated panels:** 8-10 (2 pages)

## Design Notes

- **Color palette shifts:** Cemetery scenes should be blue-black-gray. Home scenes warm amber. Metropole scenes should feel gilded and corrupt. The Borscht escape is all dark motion blur. Final walk is washed-out daylight.
- **The zombie is the visual throughline:** It appears three times -- in the cemetery, on the road, and echoed in the cab driver's story. Each appearance should use the same visual language: stark black figure, arms outstretched, blind movement.
- **Red's face as emotional barometer:** Track his expressions carefully. He goes from stone-cold (cemetery) to tender (Monkey) to sharp (deal) to desperate (escape) to hollow (surrender). This range is the chapter's engine.
- **Monkey's design:** She should be simultaneously adorable and unsettling. The golden fur, the eyes with no whites, the tiny chattering voice. She is the cost of the Zone made flesh. Every panel she's in should make the reader feel two things at once.
- **Sensory attack panel:** This is the chapter's most experimental visual opportunity. The world fracturing into geometric shapes, odors rendered as colors, space becoming solid. Go abstract for one panel.
- **Final image:** Red walking alone up an empty street. Small figure, big sky. Hands in pockets. The most restrained image in the chapter, carrying the most weight.
